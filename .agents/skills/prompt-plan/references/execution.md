# Execution

The rest of the Execution rules of [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), whose first rule holds throughout: nothing here runs until the user asks. [plan-workspace.md](plan-workspace.md) defines the folders and scripts these rules use.

- When the person an open decision names, or the user when it names none, answers it, or the user supplies a pending datum, record the answer in every fragment it governs, and in `rules.md` when it governs every change, before that fragment runs or resumes: replace the options, or the placeholder, with the answer, naming who decided and the date, and write or update the checks that depend on it. When the user revises an answer a `done` fragment already carried out, the revision is a change to that fragment under the next bullet, and its new fragment goes before the first pending fragment the revised answer governs, or where the next bullet places it when none does; when the first governed fragment that is not `done` already ran its `record NN before`, that fragment carries the revised criterion and makes these updates itself when it resumes or is retried, and no new fragment is added. That fragment carries the revised criterion with its own identifier and, right after its `record NN before`, or first when the plan has no `patch/`, updates the checks and project tests of `done` fragments that assert the replaced answer, so that they follow the revised answer's wording and threshold and are otherwise as strict as before; it records each correction to an `NN-check` script or `helpers` as `check/` does, with the revision as its evidence, and those updated lines and tests join its tests-before run. When that fragment is restored, or dropped at the user's request, its corrections to `done` fragments' scripts are undone, and a retry makes them again before its tests-before run. The report line of the replaced criterion keeps its last status and names the criterion that replaces it. A fragment still waiting on a decision or a pending datum is `blocked`.
- Edit only fragments that are not `done`, `rules.md`, and the index; a change to `rules.md` applies to the fragments not yet `done`, and when it would change what a `done` fragment required, it is handled as a change to that fragment. `done` fragments, their patches, and their states never change, except that the record of the last `done` fragment is redone, while that fragment is applied and no later fragment has run its `record … before`, when evidence shows it missed only files that did not exist before it and that `NN.state` does not list, such as tests written before its `record NN before`: move those files to the prompt skill's temporary location, run `revert NN`, `record NN before`, and `apply NN`, put the files back, rerun its checks, and run `record NN after`. The fragment stays `done`, its report lines cite the rerun check log, and `review/` keeps the evidence and the output as `NN-record-fix-<UTC timestamp>`, which the report names. When `revert NN` refuses or reports the fragment already reverted, put the files back; when `record NN before` or `apply NN` fails, put them back, mark the fragment `failed`, and stop until the user resolves it, recovering it with `apply NN`, never `record NN restore`, which refuses a recorded fragment. When the redo does not apply or stops so, report the missed files as ⚠️ outside every patch: the reviews and the closure audit read them with the patches, and a `--baseline` run moves them aside while the fragments are reverted and puts them back after `apply all`. A change to what they require becomes a new fragment placed before the one holding the closure audit, or, when that one is running, `done`, or already has its `record NN before`, a new fragment after it that repeats the final run and the closure audit; a large change becomes a new plan. A fragment added before others, or a `blocked` one moved after others, renumbers itself and the fragments after it, none of which has a record yet: their build files and scripts, the fragment number each script passes to `lib`, the index, and the dependencies follow the new numbers, while evidence already saved keeps its name. A correction made while a later fragment runs, such as one the closure audit or its reviews ask for, stays in that fragment's patch even when it touches files a `done` fragment changed, since the patches still apply in order, unless it needs a comparison with the tree before the applied fragments, which goes to a new fragment as `check/` says. Any other change to a `done` fragment, its patch, or its state that no rule of this bullet or the previous one names stops the execution, reported as a ⏸ line, with the running fragment, or else the next pending one, `blocked` in place, never moved, until the user decides.
- Take fragments in order, and stop at the first `failed` or `blocked` one until the user resolves it, since `apply` and `revert` count fragments in order; a `blocked` fragment that has no `record NN before` and that no later fragment depends on is moved instead after the fragments that do not depend on it, renumbering it and the fragments after it, none of which has a record yet, so those fragments proceed; a fragment blocked after its `record NN before` stays in place. For each, after any dry run or `--baseline` run that the dry-run rule or `check/` requires before it, first run `record NN before` when the plan has `patch/` and the fragment has none yet, before writing any of its tests or changes to project files, so its patch captures them, while a fragment resumed after `blocked` or retried after `failed` keeps the one it already ran; then, with or without `patch/`, check whether the completed fragments already deliver part of its change; when they do, turn those tests and check lines into guards, editing this pending fragment, its tests, and its `NN-check` script while keeping each criterion's wording and threshold and never making a test or check more permissive, cite the evidence, such as a test, a check line, or a log of a completed fragment, and report it. Then add its tests, if it carries any; run, under the limits `check/` scripts follow, those that test the behavior it changes, if any, together with the guards it adds, tests of behavior that already holds before it and that it keeps unchanged, written against an interface that exists before it, which do not count as tests of the change, while a test of behavior it restores or extends, such as a regression the closure audit found, is a test of the change; store that run in `review/` as `NN-tests-before-<UTC timestamp>`; if any test of the change already passes without such evidence, or a guard fails, stop without running `record NN after`, report it, and mark the fragment `blocked` until the user decides. A fragment whose changed behavior a check measures, alone or with tests, runs the lines of that check that measure it as part of its tests-before run, and that `NN-check` log is the evidence. A fragment that only measures a baseline, reruns validations, or closes the plan has no tests-before run: the first run of its check or measure script, other than the dry run, is its evidence; when it passes, the fragment changes nothing, and when a line fails, the fragment corrects it like any other, within the same limits, except that a fragment that only measures a baseline changes no project file and corrects only its measure script or `helpers`, as `check/` allows. Only then implement it, run the checks of this and every completed fragment with `all NN`, adding `--slow` when the fragment changes what a slow line of an earlier fragment measures, such as what the project renders, and in the fragment that closes the plan, and correct its implementation, without changing its criteria, tests, or checks except as `check/` allows for a script shown not to measure its criterion, until they pass, within the stop conditions the improved prompt and the project's rules set; when neither limits the corrections to a fragment's implementation, the closure audit's own limit not counting, the limit is three correction cycles, each a correction followed by an `all NN` run. All of this happens between `record NN before` and `record NN after` when the plan has `patch/`, so `record NN after` runs only once the checks pass. A commit the prompt authorizes at the end of a fragment is made once its other checks pass and before `record NN after`, which a commit does not affect; rerun the checks after it. Such commits stay in history when fragments are reverted later. Right after each `record NN after`, list the files that differ between the previous fragment's `afterTree` and this fragment's `beforeTree`, as their `NN.state` files keep them, for example with `git diff --name-only --no-renames`: those snapshots leave out the plan directory and ignored files, so each file listed changed between the two records, and one this fragment's work wrote, such as a test written before its `record NN before`, is evidence for redoing that record under the rule on `done` fragments when no `NN.state` lists it, and otherwise is a change to a `done` fragment that stops the execution as a ⏸ line, as the last sentence of that rule says. Fragment `01` has no earlier record, so a file its work wrote before `record 01 before` cannot be told apart from a change made before the plan. When the sandbox blocks a tool a check needs, keep that tool's home and caches in the plan's `.cache/` (the bundled `lib` offers it), and launch a browser through the client's one-off approval outside the sandbox; never widen sandbox settings. A fragment whose checks still fail at a stop condition is `failed` and gets no `record NN after`: its changes stay in the working tree for the user, and a retry first restores, with the user's consent, the files to the tree `record NN before` captured, through `record NN restore` when the plan has `patch/`, or as the user directs otherwise. Store the evidence in `review/`, applying the gates each fragment and `rules.md` carry and the closure audit at the end. A question a review asks is reported as a ⏸ line, in the chat and in `review/report.md`, and the execution goes on, unless a criterion cannot close without its answer, which makes the fragment `blocked`.
