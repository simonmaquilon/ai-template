# Delegation

Read before planning a normal or risk change, and before delegating to a subagent.

- In a normal or risk change, the session discovers, writes the specification, decides, handles approvals and questions to the user, integrates the results, and reports; it delegates the rest of the work.
- Delegate broad searches, read-only analysis, and mechanical or fully specified edits to the fast tier, with every change spelled out in the brief, so work that needs no judgment does not spend the session's costlier model; delegate implementation and debugging that need judgment against the fixed specification, risk changes included, to the standard tier, and implementation that exceeds the standard tier, reviews, difficult decisions, unresolved root causes, risk-area design, and independent verification to the strong tier.
- Keep in the session only questions, trivial changes, reads of known files, single commands, edits of a few lines in at most three files per change, and edits or checks a subagent's environment cannot perform, such as agent permission files, since each fresh subagent pays a fixed cost to reload instructions and files; delegate every other implementation step of a normal or risk change, since every declared tier runs on a cheaper model or at a lower effort than the session.
- Use the agents the repository declares, as the agent-subagents `.readme/` document maps them to tiers, and run a tier the client does not map in the session itself.
- Take the lowest tier that covers each task, as the session judges from discovery and the specification, and split a task that spans tiers.
- Give the independent tasks of one tier that are ready at the same time a single brief, except independent verification and, once the session has fixed the contracts and shared files they build on, such as types and translation keys, tasks that write disjoint files and each outweigh a subagent's fixed cost, which get a brief each.
- Run independent briefs in parallel, except those that start services or seed or mutate shared local state; briefs running in parallel in one working tree run only the checks scoped to their own files, and the session runs the full checks once all return.
- A higher tier can take any lower tier's work, such as searches, mechanical edits, or implementation, when its difficulty calls for it, as the session judges; a task moves one tier up when its result fails for lack of capability or a review rejects its fix again.
- Give each subagent the complete task, its constraints, acceptance criteria, and paths, pointing to the files and lines that give context instead of copying them, and require a final message of a stated maximum length with concise findings, file paths, evidence, changes made, and open issues.
- Treat a subagent's result as evidence to verify before relying on it, through the diff of what it changed or the lines its findings cite rather than whole files, and never delegate approvals, decisions owed to the user, or the final report.
